# Student Guidelines and Expectations

These guidelines set out what every research student in the group can expect from their supervisors, and what we expect in return. They apply equally to all Masters, Honours and PhD students, and we go through them together at the start of every project.

The guidelines are meant to be a shared starting point, not a contract. Part-time study, co-supervision, leave or other circumstances are agreed individually on top of them. If something here doesn't fit your situation, tell us and we'll adjust it.

This is a living document that resides at [github.com/navidcy/group-guidelines](https://github.com/navidcy/group-guidelines). If you have suggestions, feel free to tell us or open a pull request to edit it!

## Supervisor expectations

Supervision is a two-way commitment. Whatever your program, this is what we, as supervisors, commit to provide.

- **Regular, protected meeting time.** A standing meeting at an agreed frequency, which we keep and reschedule rather than cancel. Outside meetings we aim to reply to messages within a few working days.
- **A well-scoped project.** A project designed to be achievable in the time your program allows, with the background reading, data and starting code you need to begin.
- **Clear, agreed goals.** Deliverables and deadlines set together at the start of each semester (Masters/Honours) or each year (PhD), written down so we both refer to the same plan.
- **Timely, constructive feedback.** Comments on drafts and figures within a turnaround we agree on when you send them. Feedback will be direct and specific, aimed at the work, never the person.
- **Your results, not your code.** We will not review your code (unless your project has a specific model development focus). But we will review the results you obtain with it. If you think something is wrong with your code, we can suggest tests to help you track down where the bug might be.
- **Honest assessment of progress.** If we have concerns we raise them early and in person, well before any review, so nothing at a review comes as a surprise.
- **Resources and access.** Help getting set up on computing resources, software and data, and introductions to collaborators and others in the field.
- **Advocacy and development.** Support for conferences, workshops, summer schools and funding applications, references when you need them, and advice on your next step, inside or outside academia.
- **A respectful, inclusive group.** A group where everyone can ask questions without embarrassment, and where problems are dealt with fairly and confidentially.

## What we expect from every student

These apply to everyone in the group, whatever their program.

- **Treat the project like a subject or a job.** Put in consistent, scheduled time each week rather than bursts before a deadline.
- **Bring something concrete to every meeting.** A paragraph, a figure or a rough result. It doesn't need to be polished! Questions are very welcome, but questions alone don't show us what you've understood or produced. We build up to this: in the first semester of a Masters or Honours project, or the first six months of a PhD, meetings are often about reading, questions and getting set up. After that, we expect some concrete work at every meeting.
- **Keep several threads going.** Work on e.g., reading, writing, figures and code in parallel rather than one after another, so a blocked task never stops all progress. This also comes with time: early on, one focused thread is fine. After the first semester of a Masters or Honours project, or six months into a PhD, we expect at least a couple things to run in parallel.
- **Communicate early.** If a deadline is going to slip, or you're stuck, unwell or overloaded, tell us as soon as you know. Early means options are still open.
- **Act with integrity.** Follow the [University's academic integrity rules](https://academicintegrity.unimelb.edu.au/), including its guidance on using generative AI tools, and acknowledge others' code, data and ideas.
- **Be part of the group.** Attend group meetings, present your work when it's your turn, and help others where you can.

### Strongly advised

These aren't expectations as such, but they are habits we strongly recommend. They make everything above easier, especially at write-up time.

- **Write as you go.** Keep running notes and draft sections from the start; the final thesis or report is assembled, not written from scratch at the end.
- **Keep your work reproducible.** Code in version control, scripts that regenerate every figure, and data and notes organised so someone else (including future you) can follow them.

### What a good meeting looks like

1. Before: send a few lines on what you've done, what you'll show, and what you're stuck on, ideally the day before.
2. During: show the concrete work, discuss problems, agree next steps.
3. After: write down the agreed actions and share them with us, so we all start the next meeting from the same list.

## Staying in touch between meetings

Between meetings, get in touch however suits the occasion: Slack, email, a Zoom call, or a chat in person. If you're stuck, don't wait for the next meeting. We don't expect replies outside working hours, and if you haven't heard back, a nudge is always welcome.

## Masters and Honours students

A Masters or Honours research project is a subject like any other: it carries credit points, deadlines and an assessment of progress. These expectations are designed so that, by the end of each semester, both you and we can point to clear evidence of steady work.

### Time commitment

We expect the time and effort you put into the project to match your other subjects of the same credit weighting, including meeting deadlines. Plan regular hours for it each week, the way you would for lectures and assignments.

If you are struggling with the workload, raise it with us **before** the [census date](https://students.unimelb.edu.au/course-admin/census-dates): that is the point where options are still open. The University's [special consideration policy](https://students.unimelb.edu.au/course-admin/assessment-adjustments/eligibility-and-supporting-documentation) covers the kinds of circumstances that would apply. If instead you plan to prioritise other subjects during semester and make up project time over the semester break, that could also potentially work too, but **tell us early on in the semester** and we'll agree the plan and deadlines together.

### Each semester follows the same rhythm

1. **Start of semester:** we agree on deliverables and deadlines together, covering the main pieces (writing, figures, results). We write them down.
2. **Shortly before the census date:** at least one agreed deliverable falls due, followed by a meeting held regardless of how things are going. That way, if anything isn't working, both you and we pick it up early, while all the options are still open.
3. **Through the semester:** regular meetings where you bring concrete work. Other deadlines can sit in between the two fixed ones.
4. **Before the end of semester:** at least one further deliverable falls due.
5. **End of semester:** the progress review, where your program has one.

If an agreed deadline needs to move, flag it early. Anything covered by special consideration is fine.

### Semester breaks

Over semester breaks we scale meetings back. There is no expectation that you do any project work during that period.

### End-of-semester progress review

Masters/Honours programs ask both supervisors and students to complete a progress review each semester. It asks us to assess your progress, record any concerns (yours as well as ours), and answer one yes-or-no question: **are you making satisfactory progress?**

We want to answer "yes". To do that honestly, by the end of semester we need to have seen:

- the deliverables we agreed, handed in by their deadlines or with an extension agreed in advance;
- regular concrete work at our meetings, so we can show how your understanding and results developed over the semester, not just where they ended up.

If those are in place, "satisfactory" is the expected outcome. The form also has space for issues you want to raise. If something is getting in the way, tell us well before the review so we can act on it rather than just record it. Anything raised later, including at the review itself, we'll act on or adjust for in the semester to come.

### The final thesis or report

- We agree on a writing timeline at the start of your final semester, with a full draft to us at least four weeks before submission so there's time for one complete round of feedback.
- Your code and data should be in a state that can be published so that the results in your thesis can be regenerated.
- **Suggestion**: figures, methods and results sections can be drafted as soon as the work behind them is done, which saves a crunch at the end.

## PhD students

A PhD is a three-to-four-year full-time research degree (pro rata if part-time), and its goal is for you to finish as an independent researcher. Our expectations shift over the candidature: we steer closely at the start, and by the final year you set the direction.

For everything about your candidature, including milestones, leave and support, start with the University's [Graduate Research Hub](https://gradresearch.unimelb.edu.au) and the Faculty of Science's [graduate research pages](https://science.unimelb.edu.au/students/graduate-research).

### Time and leave

- Full-time candidature is equivalent to a full-time job. Keep regular, sustainable hours; we don't expect evenings or weekends.
- Take your annual leave. Rest is part of doing good research. Agree dates with us in advance; see [Taking leave](https://gradresearch.unimelb.edu.au/being-a-candidate/taking-leave) for your entitlements.

### Milestones and the advisory committee

Your progress is reviewed by your advisory committee at pre-confirmation (around six months in), at confirmation (around the end of your first year), and at annual progress reviews after that. All review dates are set against your thesis due date. The [Graduate Research Hub](https://gradresearch.unimelb.edu.au) has the current requirements.

- We agree what each milestone report will contain at least three months before it is due.
- Send us a full draft of each milestone report at least two weeks before it goes to the committee.
- Your committee includes an independent chair. You can speak to them confidentially about your supervision at any time.

### Planning each year

- At the start of each year, and after each review, we agree goals together: papers, chapters, conferences and training.
- We meet one-to-one at an agreed frequency, and you attend the group meeting.
- The same norm applies to everyone: bring something concrete to every meeting.

### Publishing and authorship

- We aim for the main results of your thesis to appear as first-author papers during your candidature.
- You lead the writing, submission and response to reviewers on your first-author papers; we support each step. The first paper is always harder than the following ones; we know that and we'll help out more!
- We discuss authorship and author order at the start of every paper, following the [Australian Code for the Responsible Conduct of Research](https://nhmrc.gov.au/research-policy/research-integrity/2018-australian-code-responsible-conduct-research).
- Code involved in simulations or analysis for a paper is released in a publicly available repository; wherever possible data is also released publicly.

### Growing as a researcher

- Present your work at group meetings regularly, and at national and international conferences during your candidature. We can guide you to relevant conferences/workshops and also help find funding for these.
- Read widely and keep up with the literature in your area, not only the papers we send you.
- Opportunities to mentor Masters and Honours students, and to review code and drafts for others in the group, will come up; take them on only if you want to.
- Tutoring and teaching are welcome, and opportunities within the School come up every semester; we agree on the hours you commit to ensure they don't crowd out your research.
- From your second year, start thinking about your next step. We'll help, whether it's in academia or beyond.

### Your final year

- We agree on a thesis writing plan about 12 months before your thesis due date.
- Send us a complete thesis draft at least three months before submission, so there's time for full feedback and revision.

## When things aren't going well

Every project has difficult stretches, and raising a problem early is never held against you. The sooner we know, the more options there are.

- **Talk to us first.** Research setbacks, workload, health, or anything else affecting your work: bring it up at a meeting or ask for a separate conversation.
- **If you'd rather not raise it with us,** Masters and Honours students can contact their Course Coordinator; PhD students can contact the chair of their advisory committee or their department's graduate research coordinator.
- **Adjustments and extensions.** Masters and Honours students can apply for [special consideration](https://students.unimelb.edu.au/course-admin/assessment-adjustments/eligibility-and-supporting-documentation). PhD students can arrange leave, part-time study or extensions through the [Graduate Research Hub](https://gradresearch.unimelb.edu.au).
- **Wellbeing.** The University's Counselling and Psychological Services (CAPS) is free and confidential for all students. Looking after yourself comes before any deadline in this document.

We'll do the same in return: if we have concerns about your progress, we'll tell you directly and early, and work with you on a plan to get back on track.
