# Student and Supervisor Expectations

_Last updated: 30 October 2026_

Here, we set out what every research student in the group can expect from their supervisors, and what we expect in return.

A core set of expectations applies to all students (Masters, Honours and PhD), with program-specific sections below. We go through them together at the start of every project. They are a shared starting point, not a contract: part-time study, co-supervision, leave and other circumstances are agreed individually, and if something doesn't fit your situation, tell us and we'll adjust it.

This is a living document that resides at [github.com/navidcy/group-guidelines](https://github.com/navidcy/group-guidelines). If you have suggestions, feel free to tell us or open a pull request to edit it!

## 1. What we commit to as supervisors

Supervision is a two-way commitment. Whatever your program, this is what we, as supervisors, commit to provide.

- **Regular, protected meeting time.** A standing meeting at an agreed frequency, which we keep and reschedule rather than cancel. Outside meetings we aim to reply to messages within 2 working days.
- **A well-scoped project.** A project designed to be achievable in the time your program allows, with the background reading, data and starting code you need to begin.
- **Clear, agreed goals.** Deliverables and deadlines set together at the start of each semester (Masters/Honours) or each year (PhD), along with a high-level workplan for achieving them.
- **Timely, constructive feedback.** Comments on drafts and figures within a turnaround we agree on when you send them. Feedback will be specific and aimed at the work, never the person. Early on, we'll agree on how you prefer to receive it (see section 2.4 of [Advice and Guidelines for Students](student-advice-guidelines.md#24-giving-and-receiving-feedback)).
- **Your results, not your code.** We will not review your code (unless your project has a specific model development focus). But we will review the results you obtain with it. It is your responsibility to ensure you are confident the code is not silently producing bugs or errors. That said, if code bugs are evident from results or discussions, we will suggest tests to help you track them down.
- **Honest assessment of progress.** If we have concerns, we raise them in a tiered way: first verbally in a meeting, then in writing if the concern persists, and only then at a formal project review — so nothing at a review ever comes as a surprise.
- **Resources and access.** Help getting set up on computing resources, software and data, and introductions to collaborators and others in the field.
- **Advocacy and development.** Support for conferences, workshops, summer schools, funding applications, references when you need them, and advice on your next steps, inside or outside academia.
- **A respectful, inclusive group.** A group where everyone can ask questions without embarrassment, and where problems are dealt with fairly and confidentially.

## 2. Student expectations

### 2.1 All students

These apply to everyone in the group, whatever their program.

- **Treat the project like a subject or a job.** Put in consistent, scheduled time each week rather than bursts before a deadline or meeting.
- **Bring something concrete to every meeting.** A paragraph, a figure or a rough result. It doesn't need to be polished! Questions are very welcome, but questions alone don't show us what you've understood or produced. We build up to this: in the first semester of a Masters or Honours project, or the first six months of a PhD, meetings are often about reading, questions and getting set up. After that, we expect some concrete work at every meeting.
- **Keep several threads going.** Work on, e.g., reading, writing, figures and code in parallel rather than one after another, so a blocked task never stops all progress. Like above, this also comes with time: early on, one focused thread is fine. After the first semester of a Masters or Honours project, or six months into a PhD, we expect at least a couple things to run in parallel.
- **Communicate early.** If a deadline is going to slip, or you're stuck, unwell or overloaded, tell us as soon as you know. The earlier we know, the more options are still open.
- **Act with integrity.** Follow the [University's academic integrity rules](https://academicintegrity.unimelb.edu.au/), including its guidance on using generative AI tools, and acknowledge others' code, data and ideas.
- **Be part of the group.** Attend group meetings, present your work when it's your turn, and help others where you can. Where it suits your project, also present at national and international conferences or workshops; we'll point you to relevant ones and help find funding.
- **Keep your work reproducible.** Your code and data should be in a state that can be published, so that the results in your thesis, report or papers can be regenerated. Code used for a paper's simulations or analysis is released in a public repository and, wherever possible, so is the data.

### 2.2 Masters/Honours students

**A Masters or Honours research project is a subject like any other**: it carries credit points, deadlines and an assessment of progress. These expectations are designed so that, by the end of each semester, both you and we can point to clear evidence of steady work.

#### 2.2.1 Time commitment

We expect the time and effort you put into the project to match your other subjects of the same credit weighting, including meeting deadlines. Plan regular hours for it each week, the way you would for lectures and assignments.

If you are struggling with the workload, raise it with us **before** the [census date](https://students.unimelb.edu.au/course-admin/census-dates): that is the point where options are still open. The University's [special consideration policy](https://students.unimelb.edu.au/course-admin/assessment-adjustments/eligibility-and-supporting-documentation) covers the kinds of circumstances that would apply.

#### 2.2.2 Each semester follows the same rhythm

1. **Shortly before the census date:** at least one agreed deliverable falls due, followed by a meeting held regardless of how things are going. That way, if anything isn't working, both you and we pick it up early, while all the options are still open.
2. **Before the end of semester:** at least one further deliverable falls due.
3. **End of semester:** the progress review, where your program has one.

If an agreed deadline needs to move, flag it early. Anything covered by special consideration is fine.

#### 2.2.3 End-of-semester progress review

Each semester, Masters/Honours supervisors and students complete a progress review that asks one question: **are you making satisfactory progress?** We expect to answer "yes" if, by the end of semester, we have seen:

- the agreed deliverables, on time or with an extension agreed in advance;
- regular concrete work at our meetings.

#### 2.2.4 The final thesis or report

We agree on a writing timeline at the start of your final semester, with a full draft to us at least four weeks before submission so there's time for one complete round of feedback.

#### 2.2.5 Semester breaks for various programs

- **[Master of Science](https://study.unimelb.edu.au/find/courses/graduate/master-of-science-earth-sciences/):** Work continues between semesters; agree on any recreational leave with us in advance. See the Faculty's [Master of Science research projects](https://science.unimelb.edu.au/students/course-guide-graduate-coursework-degrees/master-of-science-research-projects) page.
- **[Master of Climate Science](https://study.unimelb.edu.au/find/courses/graduate/master-of-climate-science/):** There is no expectation that you work on the project during semester breaks.
- **[Honours](https://study.unimelb.edu.au/find/courses/honours/bachelor-of-science-degree-with-honours/):** Honours runs on its own calendar, so most breaks within it are project time; agree any leave with us in advance. See the Handbook's [Honours course structure](https://handbook.unimelb.edu.au/2026/courses/bh-sci/course-structure) and your program's entry.

### 2.3 PhD students

A PhD is a three-to-four-year full-time research degree (pro rata if part-time), and its goal is for you to finish as an independent researcher. Our expectations shift over the candidature: we steer closely at the start, and by the final year you set the direction.

For everything about your candidature, including milestones, leave and support, start with the University's [Graduate Research Hub](https://gradresearch.unimelb.edu.au) and the Faculty of Science's [graduate research pages](https://science.unimelb.edu.au/students/graduate-research).

#### 2.3.1 Time and leave

- Full-time candidature is equivalent to a full-time job. Keep regular, sustainable hours; we **don't expect and *strongly* discourage** evenings and weekends.
- **You *must* take your annual leave.** Rest is part of doing good research. Agree dates with us in advance; see [Taking leave](https://gradresearch.unimelb.edu.au/being-a-candidate/taking-leave) for your entitlements.

#### 2.3.2 Milestones and the advisory committee

Your progress is reviewed by your advisory committee at pre-confirmation (around six months in), at confirmation (around the end of your first year), and at annual progress reviews after that. All review dates are set against your thesis due date. The [Graduate Research Hub](https://gradresearch.unimelb.edu.au) has the current requirements.

- We agree what each milestone report will contain at least three months before it is due.
- Send us a full draft of each milestone report at least two weeks before it goes to the committee.
- Your committee includes an independent chair. You can speak to them confidentially about your supervision at any time.

#### 2.3.3 Publishing and authorship

- We aim for the main results of your thesis to appear as first-author papers in peer-reviewed journals during your candidature.
- You lead the writing, submission and response to reviewers on your first-author papers; we support each step. The first paper is always harder than the following ones; we know that and we'll help out more!
- We discuss authorship and author order at the start of every paper, following the [Australian Code for the Responsible Conduct of Research](https://nhmrc.gov.au/research-policy/research-integrity/2018-australian-code-responsible-conduct-research).

#### 2.3.4 Growing as a researcher

- Read widely and keep up with the literature in your area, not only the papers we send you.
- Opportunities to mentor Masters and Honours students, and to review code and drafts for others in the group, will come up; take them on only if you want to.
- Tutoring and teaching are welcome, and opportunities within the School come up every semester; we agree on the hours you commit to ensure they don't crowd out your research.
- Research cruise expeditions, summer schools, secondments and internships come up from time to time; tell us if you're interested and we'll pass on relevant ones.
- From your second year, start thinking about your next step. We'll help, whether it's in academia or beyond.

#### 2.3.5 Your final year

- We agree on a thesis writing plan about 12 months before your thesis due date.
- Send us a complete thesis draft at least three months before submission, so there's time for full feedback and revision.
